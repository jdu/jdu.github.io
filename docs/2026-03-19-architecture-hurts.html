Jeff Uren ★ 2026-03-18

Technical architecture can be pretty hard to reason through. Especially if you're in a situation where your organisation has grown organically over a long period of time.

In those situations, Architecture can look antagonistic, it can be interpreted as control enforcing ill-fitting constraints, and as a make-work project to generate reams of documentation, increasing maintenance burden.

How do you document something that's consistently and constantly changing? How do you keep an architecture role from becoming a over-blown documentation management position.

Metrics & KPIs

This is really important to internalize before you dive into anything. There's a real conflation that metrics and KPIs are proxies for problem-solving.

The problem is that people who push for metrics and KPIs, want them, because they're too far removed from the actual domain they're applicable to, in order to be able to understand the problems that those measures elide.

Measures only tell you there is a problem. They don't carry any information about why that problem exists, or more importantly, how to solve it. If you put your stock in metrics, and overly rely on them as a solution to your problems, you will never solve your problems, and worse, you may exacerbate them.

If you think your measures and KPIs are immune to this, they're not. A measure is just an indicator. A location could have a high occurrence of reported incidents of Malaria. The result is that a coordinated campaign goes out to inoculate people against the virus. Nowhere in that metric does it tell you that the reason there's a high incidence of Malaria cases because seasonal changes have meant a spike in the specific species of mosquito that carries the virus.

Without understanding the context of metric deeper, you will spend year after year, being shocked by a recurring increase in malaria rates. All your actions will be reactive, and you'll never shift to a proactive stance which prevents these things from happening in the first place.

Maintenance is an investment

So many transformation programmes, and projects are billed as one-off, get it done, then the projects over undertakings.

Maintenance is a cost and it's recurring. Investment in a platform or SaaS doesn't mean there's a final cut-off, done date. It means that you have to commit to maintaining that thing.

Hang up the books

The first thing you need to do is hang up all the books for a bit. Your TOGAF, DODAFs, SABSA, ISO standards. They all need to be put up on the shelf for a minute. They're not unimportant, but they can distract from the important aspects of an architecture function. They make documents, and models look like the important bits when they're not.
