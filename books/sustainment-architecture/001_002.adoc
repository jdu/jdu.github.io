= What is an Organisation?

Organisations are funny things. You can call just about anything an organisation these days. A group
of people who get together to play a game, even a book club is an organisation. But when we talk
about organisations in the context of business, we're talking about something a bit more specific.

== Stripping it back

When we strip away all the bits that we commonly, and misguidedly, think of as an organisation:

* A building
* Tax Status
* Legal structures
* the org chart

you're left with a collection of people maintining a shared understanding while pursuing a 
shared purpose. All these other pieces that orbit around that, are really only support structures
put into place to help the people in the orgaqnisation achieve that shared purpose.

== Defining an Organisation

We need to take the definition of an organisation seriously, because most of what this book argues
over it's many parts and chapters follows on from how we define the organisation.

Because if an organisation is fundamentally a shared understanding, then losing that understanding is 
essentially losing the organisation itself, not just its documentation, and protecting it it becomes
the actual job of architecture, not a side effect of it.
