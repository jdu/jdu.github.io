= Architecture is Stewardship


My formal title doesn't include the term "architect", take from that what you will. Although I don't
have the title, I've been doing the work of an architect for a long time, and through that time, and the longer
period of being in software engineering, design, and operations, I've come to a few conclusions about what
architecture really is, what people think it is, and what it should actually be.

Live long enough in engineering and eventually you'll become involved with various architects, and 
leading roles that are often described as "architectural". You'll see people with those titles treated with 
a kind of reverence, and in some cases even a certain amount of fear.

But as the architect in any organisation, your job isn't really to be the smartest guy in the room at 
all times. Architects aren't there to be a font of wisdom and knowledge, the purveyors of some infinite
wisdom that only they have ascended to levels capable of tapping from the wider cosmos.

== Stewardship

Being an architect is a stewardship operation. The architect is the custodian of the organisation's understanding of itself,
and of the systems built to express that understanding. Their responsibility isn't just to design technology, but to
ensure that the organisation's identity, language, and accumulated knowledge survive the departure of individuals,
teh replacement of systems, organisational upheaval, and the passage of time itself.

You're not here to produce diagrams or approve technology choices. You're here to preserve the organisation's ability
to understand itself.
